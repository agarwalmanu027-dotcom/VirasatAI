English master narrative
Four arches, four minarets and a city growing around them: Charminar gives Hyderabad one of its most recognisable images. Sultan Muhammad Quli Qutb Shah built the monument in 1591, the year associated with the founding of the city. It stood at the centre of the historic plan, making architecture part of Hyderabad's urban identity from the beginning.

Look at the balance of the square structure. Each corner carries a minaret, while the large arches open toward the surrounding streets. The design combines a sense of enclosure with movement through the city. Granite and lime mortar form part of its construction, and an upper level contains a mosque.

The smaller details matter too. Bands, carved forms and the changing relationship between arches and towers give the monument more depth than its familiar outline suggests. It rewards looking upward, then stepping back to understand the whole composition.

Many popular stories explain why Charminar was built, but its firmly documented connection to Muhammad Quli Qutb Shah and the early city already provides a powerful introduction. Think of it as an anchor in Hyderabad's changing landscape. Roads, neighbourhoods and generations have developed around this place, while the four minarets continue to connect the city's visible present with its Qutb Shahi beginnings.
