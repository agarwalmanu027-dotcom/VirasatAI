English master narrative
At the Reserve Bank of India's Monetary Museum, a small object can open a large historical question. What gives money its value, and how have people exchanged it across time? Located in Amar Building in Mumbai's Fort area, the museum was inaugurated by President A. P. J. Abdul Kalam in November 2004.

Its collections explain the development of money through coins, banknotes and other financial instruments. A coin's metal, symbols and inscriptions can reveal the authority that issued it. A banknote tells a different story about trust, banking and the systems that support everyday exchange.

The museum's purpose is to document and present India's monetary heritage. It helps make an often invisible part of history easier to understand.

As you consider the displays, think about the money in your own pocket. Familiar objects can carry evidence of trade, technology, political power and changing economic life.
