English master narrative
Sewri Fort faces Mumbai's eastern waterfront from a small hillock. Built by the British in 1680, it belonged to a defensive landscape designed to watch the approaches to the city. Its scale is modest, but its position explains its purpose.

The structure is made largely of black basalt. An arched entrance, fortification walls, sea-facing bastions and a vaulted corridor show an architecture concerned with protection rather than royal display. Look at how the material and the arrangement of openings suit that role.

The fort also helps connect Mumbai's urban history with its coastal geography. A harbour required observation and defence, not only docks and commercial buildings.

Conservation interest in Sewri reminds us that military outposts are part of the city's heritage too. Understanding a place like this begins with reading its location, its surviving fabric and the practical task it was built to perform.
