Hampi Monuments, Karnataka — story content

Welcome to Hampi, where the remains of a great city share the landscape with granite boulders and the Tungabhadra River. This was a capital of the Vijayanagara Empire, whose rulers developed temples, palaces, markets and water systems between the fourteenth and sixteenth centuries.

Hampi is not a single building. Its story unfolds across a large landscape. The Virupaksha Temple preserves a continuing tradition of worship, while the Vitthala complex is known for its remarkable stone craftsmanship. Royal structures, gateways and street remains reveal other parts of urban life.

Look beyond the decorated pillars. Tanks and channels show how the city managed water. Long market streets explain how commerce developed around important temples. The relationship between buildings, river and rocky ground is part of what makes this place distinctive.

The city suffered major destruction after the conflict of fifteen sixty-five. Its surviving remains became evidence of both remarkable prosperity and profound change. In nineteen eighty-six, the group entered the UNESCO World Heritage List.

As you explore, allow individual ruins to connect into a larger picture. Hampi is a story of faith, power, trade and engineering, preserved not only in stone monuments but also in the landscape that shaped them.
