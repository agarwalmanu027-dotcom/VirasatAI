Ken Gharial Sanctuary — story content

Ken Gharial Sanctuary turns attention to the river as a wildlife habitat. Created to protect a stretch of the Ken, it is associated especially with the gharial, the long-snouted crocodilian adapted to catching fish.

The sanctuary was notified in nineteen eighty-one. Its riverbanks and neighbouring forest matter together, because an animal's needs extend beyond the water where we may hope to see it.

Government conservation evaluations point to the importance of suitable sand banks and to pressures on this narrow river habitat. Protection therefore involves the whole river setting, not simply counting animals.

Observe quietly from authorised areas and let guides decide appropriate distances. A wildlife sighting is a possibility, never a promise. The Ken sanctuary adds conservation to a heritage journey, reminding us that a river can be valuable for the lives it supports as well as for the scenery it creates.

Editorial notes
Wildlife sightings cannot be guaranteed. The current number of gharials is excluded because it changes and historical evaluations are not a live count.
