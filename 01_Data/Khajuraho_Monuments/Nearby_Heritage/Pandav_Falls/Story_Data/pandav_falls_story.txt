Pandav Falls — story content

Pandav Falls bring water, rock and a remembered story together in the Panna landscape. A tributary of the Ken River feeds the cascade, which drops into a pool surrounded by wooded slopes.

Near its base, caves overlook the water. Local tradition connects them with the Pandavas during their exile in the Mahabharata. That association explains the cultural memory of the place, while the waterfall and caves form its visible landscape.

Notice the change between the upper stream, the falling water and the pool below. Each part has a different movement and shape. The surrounding forest belongs to the same natural system.

Use permitted paths and follow forest guidance, especially when water conditions change. Pandav Falls invite us to understand how a natural place can hold both ecological importance and a story remembered by the people who visit it.

Editorial notes
Pandava association is an epic/local tradition rather than a verified archaeological event. No swimming or unrestricted cave access is promised.
