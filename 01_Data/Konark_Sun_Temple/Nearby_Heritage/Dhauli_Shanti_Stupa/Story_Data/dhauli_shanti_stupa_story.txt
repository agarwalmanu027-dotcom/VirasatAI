Dhauli Shanti Stupa — story content

Dhauli Shanti Stupa is a monument to peace in a landscape associated with war. The white Peace Pagoda stands on the Dhauli hills near Bhubaneswar, overlooking the setting of the Daya River.

The stupa was built in nineteen seventy-two by the Japan Buddha Sangha and the Kalinga Nippon Buddha Sangha. It is a modern memorial, separate from the ancient rock edicts connected with Ashoka in the Dhauli area.

Its meaning draws on the memory of the Kalinga War and the change associated with Ashoka's rule. The setting encourages reflection on how remembrance of violence can become a call for peace.

Notice the pagoda's form and the landscape around the hill. Respect visitors using the space for quiet thought and worship. Dhauli connects different periods of history: ancient inscriptions and a much later peace monument. Understanding that distinction makes both the site and its message clearer.

Editorial notes
The stupa is not an ancient building commissioned by Ashoka. Battlefield details and the river-running-red story are not presented as verified events. Narration distinguishes memorial interpretation from construction history.
