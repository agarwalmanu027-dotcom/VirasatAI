Kuruma Buddhist Site — story content

Kuruma Buddhist Site reveals a quieter chapter of the Konark region. In this village, archaeological remains identify a Buddhist monastery, or Buddha Vihar, alongside traces of old brick construction.

Discoveries recorded by the district administration include a Buddha image in the earth-touching pose and an image of the Buddhist deity Heruka. These finds help establish a religious history that is different from the Sun Temple's monumental setting.

At an excavated place, low walls and surviving fragments can be as important as a tall building. They invite us to think about spaces where people once lived, learned and practised their faith.

Observe the remains from authorised areas and leave stones and bricks in place. Kuruma reminds us that heritage is also found in village landscapes and archaeological evidence. Its importance comes from what these remains reveal about Buddhist life in coastal Odisha, even when much of the original building has disappeared.

Editorial notes
Kuruma is the village Buddhist archaeological site, not the better-known Buddhist complexes at Ratnagiri or Udayagiri. A precise founding date, current display location of every recovered image and excavation chronology are not asserted.
