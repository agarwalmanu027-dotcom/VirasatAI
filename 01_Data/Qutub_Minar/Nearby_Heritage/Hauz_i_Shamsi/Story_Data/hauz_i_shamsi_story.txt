English master narrative
Hauz-i-Shamsi shows why water deserves a place in Delhi's heritage story. This reservoir in Mehrauli was created under Sultan Iltutmish in twelve thirty.

The tank belongs to a landscape that also includes Jahaz Mahal and the later Jharna water structures. The connections between these places help explain how stored water could influence both the practical and the recreational spaces of a settlement.

Look beyond the water surface and consider the reservoir as a planned piece of the city. Its edges and its neighbouring monuments are parts of a shared setting. Water levels and conditions can change, but the historical importance of the site remains. Hauz-i-Shamsi invites us to connect architecture with the resources people needed, and to recognise older water systems as heritage worth understanding and protecting.
