English master narrative
Zeenat-ul-Masajid in Daryaganj is associated with a Mughal princess, Zinat-un-Nisa, the daughter of Aurangzeb. Her connection brings royal women's patronage into the history of Old Delhi.

The mosque combines red sandstone with striped marble domes. Its prayer façade, minarets and courtyard create a recognisable composition, but the individual details deserve attention too. Notice how colour and repeated openings shape the building's appearance.

The site helps broaden the story of Mughal architecture beyond the best-known emperors and their palaces. Buildings could also carry the names and choices of women within the royal family. Zeenat-ul-Masajid remains important for that association as well as its architecture. View it with the care appropriate to a religious site, and allow its specific history to stand apart from similarly named monuments elsewhere in Delhi.
