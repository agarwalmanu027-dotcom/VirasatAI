English master narrative
The Red Fort is one of Delhi's most familiar landmarks, but its history extends far beyond the red walls. Shah Jahan created it as the palace fort of Shahjahanabad, his new capital.

The enclosing sandstone walls present a strong exterior. Inside, halls, pavilions and gardens reveal a more carefully arranged world of royal life. The private apartments were connected by a water channel called the Nahr-i-Behisht, or Stream of Paradise.

Notice the contrast between the defensive boundary and the detailed palace spaces. The architecture brings together Persian, Timurid and Indian traditions, showing how Mughal builders developed a distinctive courtly style. Public and private audience halls also help explain how space expressed different forms of access to the emperor.

The fort later experienced British military occupation, which changed parts of its layout and use. Its historical layers therefore include both surviving Mughal buildings and later interventions.

The Red Fort Complex entered the UNESCO World Heritage List in two thousand seven. It also includes the older Salimgarh Fort. In independent India, the Red Fort became an enduring setting for Independence Day celebrations.

This is a place where palace architecture and national memory meet. Looking closely allows the monument to become a history of changing power, skilled craftsmanship and public remembrance.

लाल किला परिसर दो हजार सात में यूनेस्को की विश्व धरोहर सूची में शामिल हुआ। इसमें पुराना सलीमगढ़ किला भी है। स्वतंत्र भारत में लाल किला, स्वतंत्रता दिवस के समारोहों की महत्वपूर्ण पहचान बना।
