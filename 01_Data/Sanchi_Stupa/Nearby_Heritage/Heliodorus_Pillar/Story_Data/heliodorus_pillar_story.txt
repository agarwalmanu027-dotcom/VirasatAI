A single pillar near Vidisha preserves a surprisingly wide connection with the ancient world. This is the Heliodorus Pillar at Besnagar, also known locally as Khamba Baba.

Its importance comes from the inscription as much as the stone. The record names Heliodorus, son of Dion, an envoy of the Indo-Greek king Antialkidas, and connects the dedication with Vasudeva. A person, a diplomatic journey and a religious act meet in one surviving monument.

When we think about ancient India, borders can make its history seem separate from the rest of the world. This pillar encourages a broader view. People travelled, rulers exchanged envoys, and ideas crossed regions.

Look at the pillar as a piece of evidence rather than simply a tall object. Its carved writing allows a named individual from the past to remain visible. Beside Sanchi's larger monuments, this modest landmark tells history on a more personal scale.
