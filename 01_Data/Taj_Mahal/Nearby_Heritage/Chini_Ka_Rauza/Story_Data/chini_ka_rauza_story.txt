English master narrative
At Chini Ka Rauza, colour tells the story. This riverside tomb in Agra commemorates Afzal Khan, a poet and scholar who served as a minister under the Mughal emperor Shah Jahan.

Built in the seventeenth century, the monument is known for Persian glazed-tile decoration. Look for the surviving blue and turquoise surfaces. They offer a different way to appreciate Mughal craftsmanship, especially if you have just seen the white marble of the Taj Mahal.

The decoration is part of the building's identity, rather than an extra detail. Even small surviving areas can help us imagine how colour once shaped the experience of the monument. Chini Ka Rauza invites you to look closely at materials, patterns and surfaces, and to discover a quieter chapter of Agra's architectural heritage.
