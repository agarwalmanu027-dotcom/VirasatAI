English master narrative
Marble Palace introduces the world of an affluent nineteenth-century Kolkata household. Associated with Rajendra Mullick and dated to 1835, the mansion combines an imposing architectural setting with a substantial art collection.

Its façade includes fluted Corinthian columns, decorative stucco and an ornamented triangular pediment. Marble flooring is central to its identity. These materials and forms show how a residence could express taste, wealth and interest in art.

Sculpture, paintings and decorative objects add to the story. They help us think about collecting as a cultural activity: objects from different traditions could be brought together within one domestic environment.

Look at the relationship between the building and the idea of a collection. Marble Palace is a reminder that Kolkata's heritage includes private patronage and household life, alongside public monuments and institutions. The mansion opens a window onto the city's historic artistic ambitions.
