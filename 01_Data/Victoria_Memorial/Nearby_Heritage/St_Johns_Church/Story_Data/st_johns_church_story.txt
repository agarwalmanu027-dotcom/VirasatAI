English master narrative
St. John's Church on Council House Street belongs to the early architectural history of colonial Kolkata. Completed in 1787 and designed by James Agg, it drew inspiration from Saint Martin-in-the-Fields in London. It also served as the city's Anglican cathedral before Saint Paul's took on that role.

The neoclassical design emphasises columns, pediments and a clear architectural order. Its brick-and-stone construction led to the familiar name Pathure Girja, or Stone Church. The steeple and the memorial tablets add distinct elements to its appearance and history.

Notice how the formal exterior relates to the commemorative objects inside. A church can preserve records of individuals alongside its religious purpose, making the building a layered historical source.

St. John's invites thoughtful observation of faith, architecture and colonial memory. Its details connect a local city setting with wider British architectural traditions.
