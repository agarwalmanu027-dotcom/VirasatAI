Daulatabad Fort, earlier known as Devgiri or Deogiri, shows how a hill could become a powerful defensive system. Its origins are associated with the Yadavas in the twelfth century.

In the fourteenth century, Muhammad bin Tughluq's move of the capital from Delhi brought Daulatabad into a much larger political story. Later rulers added further layers to the site.

The most useful way to read the fort is through movement. Walls, gates, the moat and the rock-cut passage controlled how people approached the defended interior. The terrain and the building worked together.

Water tanks and storage remind us that a stronghold also had to sustain life inside. Follow authorised routes and take the climbing terrain seriously. Daulatabad reveals military architecture as a combination of position, controlled access and the practical resources needed to hold a place over time.
