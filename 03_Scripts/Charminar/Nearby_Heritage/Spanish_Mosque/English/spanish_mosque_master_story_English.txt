In Begumpet, the Spanish Mosque stands out through an architectural language different from Hyderabad's familiar domed silhouettes. Also known as Jama Masjid Aiwan-e-Begumpet, it is associated with the Paigah family and was completed in 1906.

Its popular name refers to the Moorish inspiration of the design. Slender, spire-like forms give the exterior a distinctive rhythm. Look at the outlines first, then notice how the decorative elements work together rather than treating each feature in isolation.

The building also has a clear religious purpose. Its prayer spaces belong to the traditions of a mosque, even as its outward appearance draws on a different architectural vocabulary.

The Spanish Mosque is a reminder that Hyderabad's heritage includes experimentation and exchange. Different influences could be adapted to local patronage, helping create a city whose historic buildings speak in more than one visual language.
