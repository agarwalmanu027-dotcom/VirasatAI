Welcome to the City Palace of Udaipur, a complex overlooking Lake Pichola that grew through the work of successive rulers of Mewar. Its many spaces are part of a long architectural history, rather than the result of one single building campaign.

As you move through the palace, notice how a courtyard opens the view and a corridor draws it close again. Balconies, rooms and terraces create a changing relationship between the building, the lake and the city. That movement is part of the experience of palace architecture.

The museum gives these historic spaces another purpose. Formally opened to the public in nineteen sixty-nine, it preserves collections that help explain Mewar's artistic and cultural world. Paintings can show how people imagined their surroundings. Sculptures and silver objects invite a closer look at the materials and skills valued in different settings.

A museum visit is most rewarding when we connect an object with the place around it. Look at a painted detail, then look again at the palace's own spaces. Each can help you understand the other.

The City Palace is both a striking part of Udaipur's skyline and a place of careful preservation. Its story continues through the study, conservation and sharing of the heritage held within its walls.
