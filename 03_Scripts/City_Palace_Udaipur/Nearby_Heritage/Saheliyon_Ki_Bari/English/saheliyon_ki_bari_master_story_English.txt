Saheliyon Ki Bari shows a softer side of royal design. Known as the Garden of the Maidens, it brings planted spaces, fountains and carved marble features together in Udaipur.

A garden is experienced through movement and attention. Notice how a path guides the eye towards water, and how a carved elephant or pavilion changes the character of the space around it. Shade and open views work together, inviting the visitor to pause.

The garden's appeal lies in these relationships rather than one single object. A fountain can be both a decorative feature and a centre around which the setting is arranged.

After Udaipur's large palace rooms and high hilltop views, Saheliyon Ki Bari offers another scale of heritage. It helps us understand that royal architecture included places of rest and pleasure, where skilled planning shaped not only buildings, but also an outdoor atmosphere.
