In Navy Nagar, the Church of Saint John the Evangelist is better known as the Afghan Church. Its familiar name comes from its memorial purpose: remembering those who died in the First Afghan War. A place of prayer and a record of loss meet within the same building.

The architecture speaks through Gothic Revival forms. Pointed arches, supporting buttresses, stained glass and a high timber roof create a strong sense of height. Local stone and carved details connect that international architectural language with the materials and workmanship of Mumbai.

Take time to follow a line from an arch toward the roof, then look back at the smaller decoration. The contrast between large structure and fine detail rewards patient observation.

The church's significance is not limited to its silhouette. It asks us to consider how communities remember the past, and how conservation can protect spaces that still carry meaning.
