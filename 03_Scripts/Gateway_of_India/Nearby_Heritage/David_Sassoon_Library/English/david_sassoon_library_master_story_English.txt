The David Sassoon Library began with a practical idea: people working at the mint and government dockyard wanted a place to share knowledge. An association formed in 1847 developed this ambition, and David Sassoon's support helped it grow into a lasting institution.

The library's Venetian Gothic building was completed in 1870. In Kala Ghoda, its architecture gives the work of reading a distinctive public face. Notice the relationship between the entrance, the reading spaces and the garden behind the building. Learning was given a setting as well as a collection.

A library is a particular kind of heritage. Its importance lies in the people who use it, the books they consult and the habits of study it supports. The historic structure makes those activities visible in the city.

This place connects craftsmanship, philanthropy and education, showing how knowledge helped shape Bombay's civic life.
