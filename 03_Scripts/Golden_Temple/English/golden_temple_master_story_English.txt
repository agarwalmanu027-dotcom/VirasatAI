Welcome to the Golden Temple in Amritsar, known to Sikhs as Sri Harmandir Sahib and Sri Darbar Sahib. Its shining shrine rises from the sarovar, the sacred pool around which the complex is arranged. A causeway connects the central building with the surrounding walkway.

The place developed through the work of the Sikh Gurus. Guru Ram Das helped establish Amritsar and its pool, while Guru Arjan developed the shrine. In sixteen hundred and four, the Sikh scripture was installed here. Later contributions, including those under Maharaja Ranjit Singh, added to the building's beauty.

Look beyond the golden surface. Cusped arches, floral work and the dome bring different forms of craftsmanship together. The water adds another dimension, reflecting the architecture while creating space around it.

The institution explains its four entrances as a welcome to people across divisions of caste, background and religion. This idea belongs to the experience of the place as much as its architecture.

As you walk, remember that this is an active place of worship. Follow the gurdwara's guidance, cover your head and treat devotees with consideration. The Golden Temple's story lives through faith, service and the people who sustain its daily religious life.
