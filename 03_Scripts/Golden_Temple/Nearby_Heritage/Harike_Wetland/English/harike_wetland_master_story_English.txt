Harike Wetland changes our view from city monuments to a living river landscape. Here, the Beas and Sutlej meet, and dammed waters form a broad wetland and shallow reservoir.

The site received international recognition under the Ramsar Convention in nineteen ninety. Its importance comes from the habitat it provides for waterbirds and aquatic life, as well as the water and livelihoods connected with it.

Watch the relationship between open water, vegetation and the edges of the wetland. A bird's presence depends on this whole environment, not simply on a patch of water.

Harike also faces pressures including siltation, pollution and invasive aquatic plants. Quiet observation and keeping litter out of the habitat support a responsible visit. This place reminds us that regional heritage includes natural systems which sustain both wildlife and communities.
