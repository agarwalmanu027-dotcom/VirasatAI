Anegundi Village extends the Hampi journey across the Tungabhadra River. This historic settlement has a story older than the great imperial city, with temples, fortifications and community life sharing the same landscape.

Its surroundings are associated with Kishkindha in Ramayana tradition. These religious connections sit alongside the physical evidence of later buildings and settlement. Keeping the two layers distinct helps us appreciate both.

Gagan Mahal, local shrines and surviving fortifications show that Anegundi was more than a background to Hampi. It had its own places of power, devotion and everyday activity.

Unlike an isolated ruin, a village is also someone's home. Respect residents and ask before photographing people or private spaces. Anegundi reminds us that heritage can continue through living communities, whose streets and practices connect historical places with the present.
