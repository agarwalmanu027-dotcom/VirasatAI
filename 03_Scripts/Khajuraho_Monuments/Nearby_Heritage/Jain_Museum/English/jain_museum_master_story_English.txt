Khajuraho's Jain Museum adds focused interpretation to the Jain temple landscape. Also known as Sahu Shanti Prasad Jain Kala Sangrahalaya, it presents sculptures and objects connected with Jain tradition.

Figures of the twenty-four Tirthankaras are an important part of the campus display. These spiritual teachers occupy a central place in Jain belief. Looking at the images with their descriptions helps turn a row of sculptures into a more understandable religious history.

Notice how similar visual forms can carry different identities. Details and museum labels deserve time; they prevent us from treating every sculpture as interchangeable.

This is a separate museum, distinct from the Jain gallery of Khajuraho's ASI museum. It offers a useful companion to the eastern temples, connecting the craftsmanship you see outdoors with the meanings and traditions that gave that art its purpose.
