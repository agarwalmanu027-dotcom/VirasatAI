Chausathi Yogini Temple at Hirapur offers a striking change from a tall, roofed temple. Its circular enclosure is open to the sky, placing the surrounding sculptures and the space above them in one experience.

Official tourism literature identifies this as a ninth-century shrine. The Yogini figures occupy niches around the enclosure, with individual sculptural details that reward patient observation.

The temple belongs to a religious tradition associated with Yoginis and the worship of feminine divine power. Its arrangement makes the whole circle important, rather than directing attention only towards one towering structure.

This is the Hirapur shrine near Bhubaneswar, distinct from Ranipur Jharial in another part of Odisha. Follow the site's customs and avoid touching the images. Hirapur reminds us that sacred architecture can take many forms. Here, a compact circle and the open sky create a memorable setting for understanding a distinctive strand of Odisha's religious and artistic heritage.
