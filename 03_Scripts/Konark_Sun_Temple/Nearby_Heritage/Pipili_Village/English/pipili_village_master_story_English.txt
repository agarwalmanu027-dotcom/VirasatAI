Pipili Village makes heritage visible through colour and stitching. This Odisha craft settlement is known for appliqué, locally called Chandua, in which pieces of coloured cloth are joined to create decorative patterns.

The work appears on umbrellas, canopies, wall hangings, bags and other textile objects. Its association with the festival chariots of Jagannath connects an everyday craft skill with a much larger religious tradition.

Look beyond the bright overall effect to the edges of each shape and the stitching that holds it in place. A finished pattern depends on many small decisions about arrangement, contrast and careful work.

Ask artisans before photographing them or their work, and listen when they explain the process. Pipili is a place where heritage continues to be made rather than only remembered. Its craft links devotion, useful objects and artistic choices, while the people practising it carry the knowledge into another generation.
