Vilachery Pottery Village brings heritage into the workshop. Here, pottery and doll-making are living skills, practised by craftspeople rather than preserved only behind museum glass.

Clay becomes vessels, figures and objects with cultural meaning. The local heritage organisation also records terracotta horse-making connected with Ayyanar temple traditions. These forms show how craft can serve both daily needs and religious life.

The finished piece is only one part of the story. Shaping, drying, firing and decorating require knowledge, attention and experience. A workshop visit helps us appreciate the labour that a brightly coloured object can hide.

Ask permission before entering workspaces or photographing artisans. Supporting carefully made local work can also support the people who carry these skills forward. Vilachery reminds us that heritage is not always a famous building; it can be a craft that continues through hands and practice.
