Rao Jodha Desert Rock Park asks you to see rocky ground as a living place. Created in two thousand six beside Mehrangarh Fort, it began as an effort to restore the area's natural ecology. The project draws attention to plants adapted to dry conditions instead of judging the landscape by how green a conventional garden looks.

On a permitted trail, notice the relationship between rock, soil and vegetation. A small plant in a crevice can be as worth studying as a large tree elsewhere. Differences in shape and texture reveal several ways of coping with a demanding environment.

Restoration is an active form of care, requiring knowledge of what belongs in a landscape. Follow the marked route and any guide instructions, avoid picking plants and leave stones undisturbed. The park adds a valuable companion story to the fort: heritage includes the natural setting around a monument, and preserving it can involve repairing ecological relationships as well as conserving masonry.
