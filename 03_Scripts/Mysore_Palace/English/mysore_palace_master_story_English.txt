Welcome to Mysore Palace, also known as Amba Vilas Palace. In the centre of Mysuru, this royal landmark tells its story through arches, domes and richly decorated halls. It is closely associated with the Wadiyar rulers, whose patronage helped shape the city's cultural identity.

The present palace was built between eighteen ninety-seven and nineteen twelve. English architect Henry Irwin designed it in the Indo-Saracenic style, combining different architectural traditions within one carefully planned building. Look at the domed towers and the sequence of arches. Together, they give the palace its distinctive outline.

Inside, the details become just as important as the grand scale. The Durbar Hall draws attention to its decorated ceiling and sculpted pillars. The Kalyanamantapa, or marriage pavilion, introduces patterned floors and stained glass. Carved doors, paintings and royal objects reveal the work of many skilled craftspeople.

The palace is also connected with Mysuru's Dasara celebrations. Its ceremonial objects remind us that a royal building was a setting for public occasions as well as private life.

As you explore, follow the changing patterns of light, colour and ornament. Mysore Palace rewards a closer look: its story belongs both to the rulers who commissioned it and to the artisans who made their vision visible.
