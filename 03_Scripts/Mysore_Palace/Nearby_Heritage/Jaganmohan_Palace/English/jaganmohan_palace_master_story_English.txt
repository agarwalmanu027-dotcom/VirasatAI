Jaganmohan Palace offers another way to understand Mysuru's royal heritage. Built in eighteen sixty-one by Mummadi Krishnaraja Wodeyar, it brings architecture and art together in a single setting.

Start at the entrance. The carved doorway includes the ten incarnations of Vishnu, showing how religious imagery became part of palace decoration. The building follows a traditional Hindu architectural vocabulary, with details that reward patient observation.

Its art gallery gives the palace a second life. Works associated with Raja Ravi Varma sit within a wider collection that includes traditional Mysore painting and other Indian art. Here, royal patronage can be understood through the images and objects it helped preserve.

Rather than rushing from one artwork to the next, notice changes in colour, subject and technique. Jaganmohan Palace connects the history of a royal household with the imagination of artists, making it an important chapter in Mysuru's cultural story.
