Jayalakshmi Vilas Mansion brings royal architecture and university learning together. Dating to nineteen hundred and five, it stands within the Manasagangotri campus of the University of Mysore.

The former royal mansion later became part of the university's museum setting. Its collections give attention to Karnataka's folk traditions, including objects connected with performance, craft and daily life.

This change of use is an important part of the story. A building once associated with royal living now helps preserve knowledge about many communities. A costume, puppet or household object can become evidence of skills and customs that might otherwise receive less attention.

As you look at the mansion and its museum context, consider how different kinds of heritage meet here. Architecture preserves one history; the objects preserve others. Jayalakshmi Vilas makes space for both, connecting the city's royal past with the cultural work of education and research.
