Welcome to the Qutub Minar complex in Mehrauli. The tall tower is its most familiar landmark, but the surrounding buildings make this a much larger story of Delhi's early Sultanate architecture.

Construction began under Qutb-ud-din Aibak and continued under later rulers, including Iltutmish. Standing about seventy-two and a half metres high, the minaret narrows as it rises. Carved bands, fluted surfaces and projecting balconies break up its height. Look upward, and then look more closely at the stonework near the ground.

Around the tower are the Quwwat-ul-Islam Mosque, Iltutmish's Tomb and the Alai Darwaza. The Iron Pillar brings an older metalworking tradition into this setting. Nearby, the unfinished Alai Minar reveals an ambitious building project that never reached its intended form.

These structures belong to different moments. Their materials, inscriptions and plans help explain how the complex changed over time. The whole ensemble entered the UNESCO World Heritage List in nineteen ninety-three.

A visit here is therefore more than a photograph of one tower. It is an opportunity to compare buildings, follow historical layers and notice the work of craftspeople. Each surviving detail contributes to the identity of this extraordinary part of Delhi.
