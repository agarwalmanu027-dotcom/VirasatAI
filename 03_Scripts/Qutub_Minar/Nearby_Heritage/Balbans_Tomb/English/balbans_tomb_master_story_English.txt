Balban's Tomb may look quieter than Delhi's famous monuments, but it marks an important chapter in architectural history. The site commemorates Ghiyas-ud-din Balban, a ruler of the Delhi Sultanate in the thirteenth century.

The surviving structure includes a square chamber and arched openings built with rubble stone. Its significance lies especially in the use of the true arch, an important development in Indo-Islamic construction.

Look at the opening as a structure, rather than only as a shape. The arrangement of the masonry helps explain how builders supported the space above it. The ruins also ask us to notice what remains and what has been lost. Balban's Tomb shows that a monument need not be complete or richly decorated to preserve an important idea from the past.
