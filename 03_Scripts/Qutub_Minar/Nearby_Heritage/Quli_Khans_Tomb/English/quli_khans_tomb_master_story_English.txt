Quli Khan's Tomb tells a story of a building whose purpose changed. The Mughal tomb commemorates Muhammad Quli Khan, the brother of Adham Khan, and stands within Mehrauli's historic landscape.

Later, the structure was adapted as a retreat associated with Metcalfe and became known as Dilkusha. This change brought a colonial chapter into a place originally created for personal remembrance.

When you look at the site, think about both layers. A building can retain its earlier form while gaining new uses and meanings. That does not make its history simple; it makes careful observation more important. Quli Khan's Tomb invites us to ask how later occupants treated older architecture. It is a useful reminder that heritage is shaped by reuse as well as by original construction.
