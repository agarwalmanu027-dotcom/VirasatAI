Zafar Mahal preserves a late chapter of Mughal Delhi within Mehrauli village. The palace was originally built under Akbar the Second, and its gateway was rebuilt under Bahadur Shah Zafar.

Red sandstone, marble, arcades and rooms formed the palace's architectural setting. The gateway is an important part of its identity, showing how an entrance could express the status of a royal building.

Today, the site is best understood by looking carefully at its surviving parts. Notice the relationship between the tall entrance and the spaces beyond it. Mehrauli was connected with royal life as well as with older Sultanate monuments and religious places. Zafar Mahal adds this later period to the neighbourhood's story. It reminds us that a historic landscape can hold many centuries within a small area.
