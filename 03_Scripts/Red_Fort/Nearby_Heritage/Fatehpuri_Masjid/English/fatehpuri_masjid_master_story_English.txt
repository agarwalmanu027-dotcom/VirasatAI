At the western end of Chandni Chowk, Fatehpuri Masjid forms an important landmark in Old Delhi's street plan. It was built in sixteen fifty by Fatehpuri Begum, one of Shah Jahan's wives.

The mosque's red-sandstone prayer façade, dome and minarets frame a courtyard space. Notice how this sacred building relates to the market streets around it. Worship and trade have long shared the setting of Chandni Chowk.

The patron's identity also matters. Like other buildings commissioned by royal women, Fatehpuri Masjid adds a perspective that can be missed in an emperor-centred history. Its location helps tie the mosque to the wider plan of Shahjahanabad. A respectful visit allows you to appreciate the building while recognising its continuing religious purpose and its place within the daily life of the neighbourhood.
