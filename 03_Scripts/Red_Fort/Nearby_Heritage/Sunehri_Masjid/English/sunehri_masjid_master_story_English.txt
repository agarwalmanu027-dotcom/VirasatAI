Near the Red Fort's Delhi Gate, Sunehri Masjid adds an eighteenth-century chapter to the neighbourhood. This particular mosque is associated with Qudsia Begum and dates to seventeen fifty-one.

Three domes and two minarets shape its outline. Its smaller scale offers a useful comparison with the fort nearby. A large palace boundary expresses one kind of authority, while a mosque introduces a different architectural purpose.

Delhi has more than one mosque with the name Sunehri Masjid, so the location matters when reading its history or identifying a photograph. Here, we are looking at the monument beside the Red Fort. Take a moment to notice the composition of the façade and the balance of its domes. It preserves a later Mughal layer within a landscape usually associated with Shah Jahan.
