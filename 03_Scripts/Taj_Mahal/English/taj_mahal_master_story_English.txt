# Taj Mahal — Master Audio Story

Welcome to the Taj Mahal.

Standing beside the Yamuna River in Agra, this magnificent monument is one of the most recognizable symbols of India.

But behind its white marble walls is a story that began almost four hundred years ago.

The Taj Mahal was commissioned by the Mughal emperor Shah Jahan in memory of his wife, Mumtaz Mahal. Mumtaz Mahal died in 1631 after giving birth to their fourteenth child. Construction of the Taj Mahal began soon after, in 1632.

The principal mausoleum was completed in 1648. The remaining parts of the complex were completed over the following years.

The Taj Mahal is much more than a beautiful marble monument. Its architecture brings together Persian, Indian, and Islamic traditions. Every part of the complex was carefully planned around geometry, proportion, and symmetry.

At the center stands the main mausoleum, surrounded by four tall minarets. The monument is built mainly from white marble, brought from Makrana, while other materials came from different regions.

Thousands of skilled craftsmen contributed to the construction and decoration of the complex. Their work can still be seen in the smallest details.

Look closely at the marble, and you will find intricate floral designs created using pietra dura. In this technique, carefully cut pieces of colored stone are set into the marble.

You can also see delicate marble screens, known as jalis. These screens create beautiful patterns of light and shadow.

Arabic calligraphy adds another layer of beauty to the monument, while the cenotaphs inside the main chamber form the symbolic center of the mausoleum.

The Taj Mahal complex also includes a mosque, a guest house, a monumental gateway, and a large garden. The long water channel leading toward the mausoleum creates the famous symmetrical view of the Taj Mahal.

And then there is the Yamuna River.

From the rear of the monument, the river becomes an important part of the Taj Mahal's setting, adding another perspective to its architecture.

But the Taj Mahal has also faced the effects of time. Over the centuries, the monument experienced deterioration and damage. Restoration work was later carried out, including major restoration completed in 1908.

Today, the Taj Mahal is a UNESCO World Heritage Site and one of the finest examples of Mughal architecture.

Its beauty is not simply in its famous dome or shining white marble.

It is in the combination of history, architecture, craftsmanship, symmetry, and countless details waiting to be discovered.

The Taj Mahal is not just a monument to look at.

It is a story preserved in marble.

And every time you look a little closer, another part of that story comes to life.
