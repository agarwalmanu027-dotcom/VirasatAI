At Sikandra, Kanch Mahal offers a smaller but distinctive example of Mughal architecture. It stands near the southeast corner of the enclosure around Akbar's Tomb.

This two-storeyed building is associated with Jahangir. Its decoration used glazed tiles and coloured stone, showing that Mughal designers explored more than one way to enrich a surface. Where details survive, they help us understand the building's ornamental character.

Take time to read the structure from the outside. Notice how one level sits above another and how openings break the solid walls. A modest building can reveal a great deal about proportion, materials and craftsmanship. Kanch Mahal is best understood through its own surviving fabric. It adds another layer to Sikandra, where imperial memory and decorative architecture meet within the same historic landscape.
