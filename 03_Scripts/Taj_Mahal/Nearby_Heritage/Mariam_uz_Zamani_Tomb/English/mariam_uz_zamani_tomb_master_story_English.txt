Near Sikandra, the tomb of Mariam-uz-Zamani introduces another important figure in Mughal history. She was the wife of Emperor Akbar and the mother of Jahangir.

The tomb stands within a walled garden and incorporates an earlier building from the Lodi period. This reuse gives the site more than one historical layer. Its sandstone surfaces and carved decoration offer a different experience from Agra's large domed mausoleums.

Look at how the walls, openings and garden relate to one another. The building's quieter scale encourages attention to details that can be missed at a larger monument. It also reminds us to include royal women in the story of Mughal heritage. Here, a familiar dynasty becomes a family history, preserved through a memorial with its own architectural character.
