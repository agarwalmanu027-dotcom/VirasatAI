In Agra's Roman Catholic cemetery stands a red-sandstone monument often called the Red Taj. It is the tomb of Colonel John William Hessing, a Dutch-born soldier who served Indian rulers.

The familiar nickname comes from its resemblance to Mughal tomb architecture. A rounded dome rises above the central structure, while arches, stone screens and corner pavilions shape the exterior. Look at the warm sandstone and compare its effect with the white marble of the Taj Mahal.

This is a distinct memorial, with its own identity and setting. It reminds us that Agra's history included people who travelled across countries and worked within Indian courts and armies. As you explore, remember that you are in a burial ground. Quiet observation gives both the architecture and the people commemorated here the respect they deserve.
