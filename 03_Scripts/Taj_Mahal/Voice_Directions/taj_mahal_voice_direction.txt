TAJ MAHAL — VOICE DIRECTION

Voice Role:
A warm, calm and knowledgeable heritage storyteller.

Overall Style:
The narration should feel like a professional heritage guide telling the story directly to a visitor.

Tone:
Respectful, calm, natural and slightly emotional.
The emotion should feel genuine and subtle, not dramatic.

Speaking Speed:
Medium-slow.
Give the listener enough time to understand historical information and imagine the monument.

Delivery:
Speak naturally and clearly.
Avoid sounding like a news reader, advertisement, audiobook robot, or school presentation.

Pauses:
Use short natural pauses after important historical facts and major transitions.
Use slightly longer pauses when moving from history to architecture or from architecture to conservation.

Emphasis:
Give gentle emphasis to important names, places and concepts such as:
Taj Mahal
Shah Jahan
Mumtaz Mahal
Yamuna River
Makrana
Mughal architecture
pietra dura
jalis
UNESCO World Heritage Site

Pronunciation:
Pronounce historical names, Indian place names and architectural terms clearly.
Do not rush difficult or unfamiliar words.

Emotional Direction:
The beginning should create curiosity.
The historical section should feel reflective.
The architecture section should feel appreciative and informative.
The ending should feel memorable and inspiring.

Important:
Do not over-dramatize the story.
Do not add information that is not present in the master story.
Do not change the meaning of the script.
Keep the narration suitable for a heritage and educational application.

Language:
Apply the same voice characteristics to both English and Hindi versions.
For Hindi, maintain natural Hindi pronunciation and rhythm rather than translating the English speaking style word-for-word.
