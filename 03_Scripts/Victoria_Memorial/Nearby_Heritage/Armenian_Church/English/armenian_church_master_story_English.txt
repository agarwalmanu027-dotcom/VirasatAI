The Armenian Holy Church of Nazareth preserves the history of a community that helped shape Kolkata. On Armenian Street in Burrabazar, the church and its cemetery offer evidence of Armenian settlement within the city's early life.

The Armenian Embassy records a wooden church built in 1707, followed by rebuilding in 1724 associated with Agha Nazar. A belfry, also serving as a clock tower, was added in 1734. These stages show how a place of worship developed with the community around it.

Look at the relationship between the church and the adjacent burial ground. Faith and remembrance belong to the same setting, making the site more than an isolated architectural object.

This church introduces Kolkata through the presence of its Armenian residents. Their history reminds us that the city's heritage was shaped by many communities whose religious institutions and everyday lives remain part of its story.
