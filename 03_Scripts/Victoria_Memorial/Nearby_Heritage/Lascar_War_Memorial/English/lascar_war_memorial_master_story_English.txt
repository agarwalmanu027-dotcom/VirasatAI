The Lascar War Memorial brings seafarers into the story of Kolkata's heritage. It commemorates 896 seamen of Eastern India who lost their lives at sea during the First World War. The memorial was unveiled in February 1924.

Its tall, four-sided form makes remembrance visible in the city's naval landscape. Maritime references in the design connect the monument with the work of the people it honours. Indian architectural details add another layer to its character.

The word lascar was used for seamen from the Indian subcontinent. Their labour supported long-distance shipping, yet their names and contributions can be less familiar than those of political or military leaders.

This monument asks us to widen our view of history. A port city's past includes the people who worked on its ships, faced danger far from home and helped connect local life with the wider world.
