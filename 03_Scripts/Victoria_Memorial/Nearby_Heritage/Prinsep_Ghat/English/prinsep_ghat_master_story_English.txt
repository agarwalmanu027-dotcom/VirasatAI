Prinsep Ghat brings a commemorative pavilion into Kolkata's riverfront landscape. The monument remembers James Prinsep, a scholar and antiquarian whose work belongs to the study of India's past. Designed by W. Fitzgerald, the Palladian porch was erected in 1843 with civic contributions and government support.

Look at the measured arrangement of columns and openings. The neoclassical design creates a formal rhythm, while the riverfront setting places the monument beside a landscape of movement and change.

The ghat can be understood through that relationship. A memorial honours an individual, but its location also connects it with the broader history of a city shaped by the Hooghly.

Take time to see the pavilion and river as parts of one scene. Prinsep Ghat offers a way to think about scholarship, public remembrance and the spaces a city chooses to preserve.
